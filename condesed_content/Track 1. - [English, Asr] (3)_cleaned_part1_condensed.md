## Attendance Expectations

- Attendance is not directly assessed.
- Students are not penalized for missing an online session due to work, illness, or other commitments.
- Students should still keep up weekly by watching recordings if they miss a live session.
- Students enrolled in an in-person workshop are encouraged to attend in person because:
  - A physical place is reserved for them.
  - Other students cannot take that place.
  - In-person discussion with classmates and tutors can improve learning.
- If an in-person workshop time does not suit a student, they can contact staff about switching or freeing the place.
- Students enrolled online can attend workshops online and complete the same quiz requirements.

## Assessment Structure

### Weekly Quizzes

- Weekly quizzes are worth 40% total.
- There are 8 assessed weekly quizzes.
- Each quiz is worth 5%.
- Workshop quizzes are the assessed quizzes, not the self-review quizzes.
- Workshop quizzes normally contain around 5 to 10 questions.
- Students can practice during or after the workshop.
- They do not have to submit during the workshop session.
- The quiz is due by the following Wednesday at 5:00 pm.
- Example: a quiz for week 3 is due in week 4 on Wednesday at 5:00 pm.

### Self-Review Quizzes

- Self-review quizzes are for checking personal understanding.
- They are not the assessed workshop quizzes.
- They may contain multiple-choice questions.

### Final Exam

- The final exam is worth 60%.
- It is an online proctored exam.
- During the exam students cannot use online chat with classmates or tools such as ChatGPT.
- One A4 double-sided note sheet is allowed.
- Students can prepare notes and use them in the online exam.

### Final Exam Hurdle

- The final exam has a hurdle requirement.
- Students must get more than 40% in the final exam to pass the course.
- Even if weekly quiz marks bring the total above 50%, a final exam score below the hurdle means the course cannot be passed.
- Example:
  - A student gets 40% from quizzes.
  - They get only 30% in the final exam.
  - Their total is above 50%, but they still fail because they did not meet the final exam hurdle.
- Keeping pace with weekly study and workshops should make the hurdle achievable.

## Linux, Unix, and Virtual Machines

- The course uses Linux for workshop activities.
- Students are given learning material introducing Linux and Unix history.
- Some students may choose to install Linux or virtual machines on their own computer.
- Installing a virtual machine is optional.
- The university provides a cloud service solution so students do not need to install virtual machines locally.

## Local Virtual Machine Considerations

Installing virtual machines locally may require significant resources:

- Hard drive space.
- Memory.
- Enough performance to run one or more virtual machines.

Specific notes from the lecture:

- One version mentioned may require around 20 GB of hard disk space.
- Running two virtual machines at the same time can make older computers slow or unusable.
- Newer M1 and later Mac computers were described as having virtualization limitations for the course setup.
- An older Ubuntu version, such as version 16, may require fewer resources and may work better on weaker machines.

Students who want to avoid technical setup problems can use the provided cloud account instead.

## Cloud Accounts

- Cloud accounts will be provided to students enrolled in the course.
- The cloud service allows students to simulate the same activities without installing virtual machines locally.
- This avoids problems with limited disk space, memory, or local virtualization compatibility.

## What the Course Covers

The course studies:

- Who attackers are.
- What methods attackers use.
- How to protect cyberspace.

Cybersecurity is taught across three dimensions:

- People.
- Technology.
- Policy.

## Three Dimensions of Cybersecurity

### Technology

- The main portion of the course focuses on technology.
- Modules 2 and 3 are mostly technology-based.
- Students learn technical concepts and tools so they can understand how cybersecurity protections work.

### Policy

- Policy topics appear later in the course.
- The course includes two weeks focused on policy.
- Policies help ensure technology is used properly and reaches required standards.
- Policies also constrain and guide people.

### People

- People are covered later in the course.
- The course includes one week focused on the human aspect.
- People are sometimes the most important part of protecting cyberspace.
- Users need to know how to use technology correctly.
- Users need to understand and follow policy.
- Human behavior can determine whether technical protections are effective.

## Relationship Between People, Technology, and Policy

All three dimensions are equally important.

Cybersecurity works only when:

- Technology is properly designed and used.
- Policies set standards and constraints.
- People understand how to use technology and follow policy.

Technology is a major course focus because students need technical knowledge to:

- Understand threats.
- Apply protections.
- Ensure security standards.
- Improve security awareness.
- Design or follow effective policies.

## Study Discipline

Students who do not keep up may struggle because they may not understand:

Keeping pace with weekly lectures, workshops, and quizzes is important for both assessment and understanding.

# Cybersecurity Course Introduction and Motivation

## Course Approach

- This course is practice-based and application-based.
- Students are expected to understand the theory behind workshop activities, not only complete the tasks mechanically.
- To succeed in the final exam, students should:
  - Go through each week's workshop activity.
  - Understand why questions are answered in a particular way.
  - Connect practical activities to the supporting theory.
  - Learn how concepts are used in real security situations.
- The course emphasizes using knowledge in reality rather than memorizing isolated concepts.

## Course Scope and Materials

There is no single textbook that covers all required areas because cybersecurity spans multiple major domains:

- Network security:
  - Network security protocols alone can form an entire course.
  - Students without background knowledge in TCP/IP or network protocols may need extra reading.
- Cryptography:
  - Cryptography can also be a full course.
  - Many books cover large numbers of cryptographic algorithms.
  - This course focuses on the most popular algorithms and applications built on them.
- Authentication and access control:
  - These areas can also fill a whole textbook.
- Policy:
  - Security policy is another major dimension of cybersecurity.

The course provides enough learning material through:

- Lecture slides.
- Learning activities.
- Self-review quizzes.
- Course site resources.

Useful references mentioned:

- *CISSP All-in-One Exam Guide*:
  - Used as a basis for access control, authentication, and policy material.
- Cryptography reference books:
  - Used to build the cryptography content.
- SEED Labs by Professor Wenliang Du:
  - Used for some practical technical material.
  - Provides many cybersecurity labs developed over more than 10 years.

Software security is mostly excluded because it relies heavily on coding and software design. Some small code components are used only to help students understand cryptographic algorithms, and code is provided directly rather than requiring students to write it line by line.

## Why Study Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity matters because attacks and weaknesses affect software, data, devices, infrastructure, organizations, and everyday life.

### Software Vulnerabilities

- Vulnerability means a weakness in software, systems, or processes.
- Identified vulnerabilities are assigned IDs.
- Some vulnerabilities are already known; some are unknown until discovered and analyzed.
- The number of identified vulnerabilities has increased over time.
- Causes include:
  - Buffer overflow.
  - SQL injection.
  - Cross-site scripting, or XSS.
  - Cross-site request forgery, or CSRF.
- These vulnerability types are discussed later in the course.

### Data Breaches

Large breaches occur across many sectors and countries, including:

- Hotels and commercial companies.
- Social media platforms such as Facebook and Twitter.
- Governments and public-sector bodies.
- Financial institutions such as JP Morgan.
- Phone companies.
- Police and other government-related organizations.

Examples of breached data include:

- Personal data.
- Source code.
- Large volumes of user or customer records.

The severity of a breach can be represented by the number of records exposed or the financial impact.

### Cybersecurity Affects Physical Equipment

Security issues are not limited to ordinary computers.

Examples discussed:

- Uranium or nuclear-related manufacturing facilities:
  - If industrial control systems are compromised, attackers could potentially control dangerous physical processes.
- Cars:
  - Ethical hackers demonstrated attacks against vehicle operating systems.
  - Attacks can affect steering wheels, brakes, and other controls.
  - A compromised car can become unsafe while driving.
- Tesla demonstration:
  - Hackers demonstrated control over car behavior, including making cars respond to music.
- Pacemakers:
  - A security hole affected about half a million people.
  - Devices had to be taken out, patched, and returned.
- Critical infrastructure:
  - Electricity systems, water facilities, railway control systems, and other control systems can be attacked.

Cybersecurity failures can therefore affect health, safety, transport, infrastructure, and national or global security.

## Cybersecurity Career Demand

Because attacks and vulnerabilities occur across many domains, there is a large workforce shortage.

Organizations hire for roles such as:

- Security architect.
- Security engineer.
- Secure software development roles.
- Other cybersecurity positions.

Cybersecurity is multidisciplinary. Professionals benefit from broad knowledge, but students without an IT background can still begin through appropriate entry points.

Career pathways can include:

- Feeder roles.
- Entry-level cybersecurity roles.
- Mid-level roles.
- Advanced roles.

Useful career information includes:

- Average salary by role.
- Top requested skills.
- Top requested certifications.

Certifications are useful, but proper certification usually requires both knowledge and practical experience. Introductory study can support later certification.

Examples of certification organizations or certifications mentioned:

- ISC / CISSP.
- CompTIA-style advanced security certifications such as CASP.
- Certified Ethical Hacker, associated with EC-Council.
- Cisco security certifications, including more technical Cisco security pathways.

Some certifications may be treated as equivalent or comparable depending on organization and employer.

## What the Course Covers

The course uses three major dimensions:

- People.
- Technology.
- Policy.

Technology alone does not solve all cybersecurity problems.

Examples of topics and activities:

- Ransomware:
  - How ransomware attacks happen.
  - How to prevent ransomware attacks.
- RSA:
  - A fundamental cryptographic algorithm supporting secure banking.
  - Students learn what RSA is, what it does, and how it works in practice.
- Packet sniffing:
  - Students simulate attackers and defenders in a virtual machine.
  - Activities show how attackers sniff packets between users.
  - Students analyze how network protocols protect online traffic.
- Policy:
  - The course covers Australia's Essential Eight.
  - Essential Eight is an Australian government-supported cybersecurity framework.
  - Organizations may be asked to embed Essential Eight controls.

## Why Cybercrime Exists

Cybercrime exists when three factors align in the MAO framework:

- Motivation.
- Ability.
- Opportunity.

If all three are present, cybercrime is likely to occur.

### Motivation

Attackers may want:

- Money.
- Data.
- Control.
- Other benefits from compromising systems or users.

### Ability

Ability does not always require advanced technical skill.

- Attackers can buy hacking tools from black markets or "dream markets."
- Ransomware and other attack tools may be purchased and launched with limited expertise.
- Cryptocurrency such as Bitcoin may be used for payment.

### Opportunity

Opportunity exists when targets are vulnerable.

Examples:

- A user clicks any link in an email.
- A system has unpatched vulnerabilities.
- Users or organizations lack cybersecurity awareness.

## Why Attackers Often Win

Cybersecurity is asymmetric:

- Defenders must close every possible window.
- Attackers only need one small hole to break in.

Defense must account for:

- The system itself.
- People and their cyber awareness.
- Vulnerabilities across all access points.

Additional reasons cybercrime persists:

- Existing systems may already be insecure.
- People remain vulnerable to deception and mistakes.

To protect data, networks, and systems, defenders must understand:

- Who attackers are.
- Their motivation.
- How they get in.

This resembles the idea from *The Art of War*: to win, one must understand the opponent.

## Types of Hackers

Common hacker categories:

- Black hat:
  - Malicious or criminal hackers.
- White hat:
  - Ethical hackers who work to identify and fix security problems.
- Gray hat:
  - Hackers whose behavior falls between black-hat and white-hat categories.

# Lec1Rec2: Cybersecurity Terms, Risk, Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Exploits

## Why These Terms Matter

Several cybersecurity terms are commonly confused:

- Risk
- Threat
- Vulnerability
- Exploit

These terms have different meanings and must be used correctly in cybersecurity documentation, including:

- Risk assessment reports
- Security policies

## Threat

A **threat** is an agent or actor that may cause damage.

A threat can be:

- A human
- An object
- A substance
- Anything that may act in a way that causes harm

Examples:

- A drunk driver who may cause a car crash
- A virus inside a computer
- A hacker who may attack a system

To decide whether something is a threat to a network or system, we first need to understand the assets in that network or system. Threat identification depends on knowing what needs protection.

## Assets and Asset Inventory

An **asset inventory** is a list of all assets in an organization.

It should include assets such as:

- Hardware
- Software
- Applications
- Customer data

Organizations must identify which assets are most valuable. Valuable assets may include:

- Intellectual property
- Trade secrets
- Credit card payment information
- PII, meaning **personally identifiable information**

PII may include information such as:

- First name
- Driver's license information

After assets are identified and prioritized, negative impacts against those assets can be analyzed.

## Types of Threats

Threats can be classified as:

- Known threats
- Unknown threats
- Advanced persistent threats

### Known Threats

A **known threat** may be malware with a unique virus signature.

Known threats can often be detected because their identifying characteristics are already recognized.

### Unknown Threats

An **unknown threat** is usually a very new threat that cannot yet be detected by antivirus software.

Unknown threats are sometimes called:

- Zero-day threats

### Advanced Persistent Threats

An **advanced persistent threat** involves a long-term effort to:

- Gather information
- Compromise a system
- Cause more severe damage

## Vulnerability

A **vulnerability** is a weakness in a system or network that allows a threat source to compromise security.

Examples of vulnerabilities include:

- Outdated firmware in hardware
- An unlocked computer that can be stolen easily
- Poorly designed software
- A careless employee who does not properly log out from a critical financial data management system

These weaknesses may be exploited by a threat actor.

## Exploit

An **exploit** is the process or behavior of engaging with or taking advantage of vulnerabilities.

An exploit occurs when a threat actor uses a vulnerability to compromise or harm a system, network, or asset.

## Risk

**Risk** is related to:

- The probability that a threat will exploit a vulnerability
- The impact of that exploit

Risk can be expressed as:

```text
Risk = Probability x Impact
```

The likelihood is high when:

- A vulnerability exists
- A threat actor has the ability to exploit that vulnerability

## Risk Example: Careless Employee and Hacker

Scenario:

- A careless employee saves a password in an open, unencrypted file.
- A hacker is trying to hack into the employee's computer.
- The employee is a financial officer.

Analysis:

- The saved password is a weakness.
- The hacker is the threat agent.
- The hacker can exploit the careless employee's bad habit.
- The likelihood of compromise is high because the password is exposed.
- The impact is severe because the employee has access to financial systems.

Result:

- The overall risk is very high.

In this example:

- **Threat agent:** the hacker
- **Threat:** the harm the hacker may cause to the company
- **Vulnerability:** the careless employee's insecure password storage habit
- **Exploit:** the hacker taking advantage of the exposed password
- **Asset at risk:** the financial data management system
- **Possible damage:** exposure of important data

A security officer must identify safeguards to protect assets against threats.

## Motivation, Ability, and Opportunity Model

The cybersecurity risk model can also be described using three factors:

- **Motivation**
- **Ability**
- **Opportunity**

These factors are linked to cybersecurity concepts:

- **Motivation** is linked to threat actors.
- **Ability** is linked to exploits.
- **Opportunity** is linked to vulnerabilities.

Risk emerges when an attacker has:

- Motivation to attack
- Ability to exploit
- Opportunity because a vulnerability exists

When motivation, opportunity, and ability exist at the same time, there is likely to be a resulting behavior. This model helps explain why cybercrime continues to exist in cyberspace.

In cybersecurity, the course focuses on learning how to:

- Identify cyber attacks
- Protect cyberspace
